# Research knowledge

## Source hierarchy (weight claims accordingly)

1. Primary sources: official docs, standards, papers, court filings, changelogs.
2. Vendor announcements and release notes (primary for product facts, biased for comparisons).
3. Reputable secondary analysis (name the author and date; check for conflicts of interest).
4. Forums and Q&A: useful for pointers and failure modes, not for facts.

Never cite a source you did not actually read; fetched-and-read ≠ search-result title.

## Method: gather → triangulate → synthesize

- **Gather**: define the question precisely; collect 3+ independent sources per key claim; record URL + access date.
- **Triangulate**: a fact confirmed by independent sources is reportable; a single-source claim is labeled as such; contradictions are reported, not resolved silently.
- **Synthesize**: answer the question first, then the supporting structure; separate *facts* (cited), *inferences* (derived), and *unknowns* (open).

## Recency and context checks

- Check publication/update dates; software facts older than ~12 months need re-verification.
- Prefer version-pinned facts ("as of v2.3") over timeless-sounding claims about moving targets.
- Note jurisdiction/locale for legal, tax, and regulatory topics.

## Citation discipline

- Every factual claim maps to a source; every quote maps to a location.
- Distinguish "source says X" from "I infer Y from X" — never blend them.
- When sources conflict, present the strongest version of each position with sources.

## Anti-patterns

- Accepting SEO-optimized content farms as evidence.
- Treating absence of search results as proof of absence ("I couldn't find it" ≠ "it doesn't exist").
- Copying long excerpts — summarize with citations instead (respect copyright).

## Agent-specific pitfalls

- `web_fetch` output is the evidence — cite the URL you actually fetched.
- Stop researching when marginal sources stop changing the answer; report remaining unknowns.
